What can I claim as an SJP adviser?

Almost certainly your biggest claim

Mileage. 45p a mile to 10,000 business miles, 25p after. Every client visit, introducer meeting, seminar, networking event. Not your commute. Log it the same day; a reconstructed log at year end is the first thing HMRC challenges.

Straightforward

Professional fees and training. CII/PFS membership, exam entries, study materials, CPD events. Training that maintains or updates existing skills is deductible.

Regulatory and practice costs you personally bear. If the practice recharges you for software, PI contribution or marketing, it's deductible. If the practice pays it, it isn't yours to claim.

Phone and internet. The business proportion, applied consistently.

Home working. The flat rate is simplest: £10 a month for 25–50 hours, £18 for 51–100, £26 for 101+. Set it in Settings and it's added automatically.

Travel other than by car. Train fares, parking, tolls, taxis on business journeys. Parking fines never.

Subsistence. Reasonable meals only when travelling away from your normal base on business. Lunch near the office is living, not trading.

Accountancy, software, stationery, printing, postage.

Not deductible, however tempting

Client and introducer entertaining. Coffees, lunches, gifts to clients. Record them so your accountant has the full picture, but they never reduce your tax.

Clothing. Suits are not deductible even if only worn for work.

Fines, personal insurance, pension contributions. Pension contributions get relief through your return, not as a business expense. See the Tax tab for how much that's worth.

Mileage

Business journeys only: client meetings, introducers, seminars, training. Your regular trip to the Royston office is commuting and does not count. 45p per mile for the first 10,000 business miles in the tax year, then 25p.

How self-assessment payments work. The bill for a tax year is settled on 31 January after the year ends. If it's £1,000 or more, HMRC also asks for two payments on account towards the following year, each half of this year's income tax and Class 4 NI, on 31 January and 31 July. So the January after a good year is the expensive one: the balance for the year just gone, plus the first instalment for the next. Student loan repayments are settled with the balance but aren't included in payments on account.
Pension: turning tax into savings

Contribution planner

A personal pension contribution paid from your own money gets 20% added by HMRC in the pension, and extends your basic-rate band by the gross amount so that higher-rate tax falls away through your return. Class 4 NI isn't reduced. Slide to see the effect.
