What to claim as an SJP adviser

The short, honest list for a self-employed adviser with a small cost base. The test for everything is "wholly and exclusively for the business".

Almost certainly your biggest claim

Mileage. 45p a mile to 10,000 business miles, 25p after. Every client visit, introducer meeting, seminar, networking event. Not your commute. Log it the same day; a reconstructed log at year end is the first thing HMRC challenges.

Straightforward

Professional fees and training. CII/PFS membership, exam entries, study materials, CPD events. Training that maintains or updates your existing skills is deductible; training that qualifies you for a wholly new trade is not, but for an adviser doing adviser exams that distinction is rarely a problem.

Regulatory and practice costs you personally bear. If the practice recharges you for anything (software licences, PI contribution, marketing) it's deductible. If the practice pays it, it isn't yours to claim.

Phone and internet. The business proportion. Be honest and consistent: if it's 60%, claim 60% every month.

Home working. The flat rate is simplest: £10 a month for 25–50 hours, £18 for 51–100, £26 for 101+. Set it in Settings and it's added automatically. If you have a dedicated office and significant costs, an actual-cost apportionment may be higher; ask NB.

Travel other than by car. Train fares, parking, tolls, taxis on business journeys. Parking fines never.

Subsistence. Reasonable meals only when travelling away from your normal base on business, typically an overnight stay or a full day away. Lunch near the office is living, not trading.

Accountancy, software, stationery, printing, postage. Including NB's monthly fee and this platform's hosting when it has any.

Not deductible, however tempting

Client and introducer entertaining. Coffees, lunches, drinks, gifts to clients. Record them so your accountant has the full picture, but they never reduce your tax bill. The one exception is a business gift under £50 carrying a conspicuous advert for your business, which for an adviser almost never applies.

Clothing. Suits are not deductible even if you only wear them for work.

Fines, personal insurance, pension contributions. Pension contributions get relief through your tax return, not as a business expense; they still belong in your planning, just not here.

Record-keeping rules that now bite

Under MTD each transaction needs a date, amount and category, held digitally at the time. Keep receipts as photos alongside; a folder per quarter in your phone is enough. Bank statements every six months no longer satisfy the requirement, which is why this file exists.

Settings & backup

Records sync to your account on every change and open on any device you sign in on. Still download a backup at each quarter end and keep it in your accounts folder; a file you hold is the record HMRC can never be told you lost.
